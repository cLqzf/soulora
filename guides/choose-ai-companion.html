Choosing a companion

How to choose an AI companion.

Start with the conversation you want to have. Then check what an app actually remembers, how it speaks, and how much control you keep.

Start with the kind of company you want

An AI companion is software designed for ongoing conversation with a simulated personality. The same search can lead to everyday chat, fictional roleplay, voice tools, or general-purpose assistants. They do not necessarily solve the same problem.

Write down one ordinary task: talking through your day, continuing a fictional story, or having a hands-free conversation. Use that task when comparing products, rather than judging only their character images or opening messages.

  • Everyday conversation: look for a tone you can change, continuity across sessions, and room to disagree or end a conversation.
  • Roleplay: check character setup, story context, and the product's content boundaries.
  • Voice companionship: try a real conversation, including pauses and interruptions, on the device you plan to use.

For examples of different entry points, Zeta's public site presents character and story discovery, while Animates' public site presents phone and desktop download options. These are examples, not recommendations or a performance comparison. Check each provider's current details before deciding.

Ask what “memory” actually means

Continuing the current chat, recalling previous chats, and saving a preference are different jobs. A product saying it has memory does not, by itself, explain which of those jobs it performs or how you control it.

  • Can you see the information it has saved about you?
  • Does it ask before saving, or does it save automatically?
  • Can you correct an inaccurate memory without deleting everything?
  • Can you remove one memory, and is that separate from clearing chat history?
  • What happens when memory is off, you change devices, or you cancel a subscription?

A simple test: share a made-up, non-sensitive preference, such as a fictional favourite colour. Check whether you can find, change, and remove it using documented controls. A reply saying “I've forgotten” is not a substitute for a clear deletion process.

Test the language you actually use

A translated website is not proof of fluent chat, voice recognition, or speech in that language. Check those capabilities separately. This matters whether you speak English, Japanese, Korean, Thai, Spanish, or Portuguese.

Try a short everyday exchange using your normal vocabulary. If you switch languages, test that too. Ask whether voice is included in the same plan, and whether support and cancellation instructions are available in a language you can read.

For voice, listen beyond the sample

Check whether you can pause, interrupt, change the speaking pace, and clearly stop the microphone. Test on your usual connection and device. A polished recording does not show how a live call handles a long pause or a misunderstanding.

Understand the data before sharing it

Look for an identifiable operator, contact information, and a privacy policy that describes the actual service. If you cannot find an answer, treat it as unknown rather than assuming the most favourable interpretation.

  • What conversation, voice, and account data is collected?
  • Is data used for model training, and what choices are available?
  • Which providers receive it, and how long is it retained?
  • How do you export data or delete history, saved memories, and the account?
  • Do the rules and controls apply to your plan and location?

Use non-sensitive examples while evaluating a product. An AI persona is not a real person, and a companion app should not be treated as a substitute for professional care or real-world relationships.

Check the complete path to using it

Confirm that the product is available on your device and in your region. Follow the provider's official store or download links, check system requirements, and find out whether a web account and app account share the same plan.

Read the actual limits: messages, voice minutes, memory, images, and any extra purchases. Check billing frequency, renewal terms, and where cancellation happens. “Free to download” does not explain the cost of the experience you want.

If a product is still a preview, separate what you can try today from a roadmap or waitlist promise. Do not enter payment details simply to discover whether the advertised capability exists.

Use the same small trial for each product

Keep a short note for each candidate: the date, device, plan, and what you actually observed. This makes your comparison more useful than a single impressive answer.
